Each compares an incoming contract with your preferred positions and fallbacks, then proposes redlines for a lawyer to accept or reject. They differ mainly in who builds the playbook, whether the work happens inside Microsoft Word or a separate web app, how sign-off and audit trails are handled, and how openly they publish pricing. ## Key takeaways

- **Playbooks are the core mechanism.** As [Axiom explains](https://www.axiomlaw.com), most AI contract review software compares an incoming document against the company's own playbook.
- **Word is the default workspace.** Gavel Exec, Spellbook and BlackBoiler centre on Word; LegalOn pairs Word with its own web app.
- **Pricing is often reported, not published.** Only some vendors list prices; several figures below come from competitors and should be checked on each vendor's own pricing page.
- **Human review is still required.** A qualified person reviews every issue and decides. The tools do not give legal advice.

## How does AI contract review against a legal playbook work?

A legal playbook records your organisation's standard position on each clause, the fallbacks you will accept, and the points where you walk away. Playbook-based AI applies those rules to a third-party draft.

### Positions, fallbacks and redlines

The tool reads the contract, maps each clause to a playbook issue (limitation of liability, indemnities, governing law, payment terms and so on), flags deviations, and proposes a redline that moves the clause back towards your preferred or fallback position. Better tools also explain why each change was suggested, so the reviewer can check the reasoning rather than accept text blindly.

### Where the human stays in the loop

Every vendor in this category positions its output as suggestions for a qualified reviewer. The practical questions are how that review is recorded, who can approve a redline before it is sent, and whether there is an audit trail showing what the AI proposed and what a person decided. Those questions matter more as AI review scales from a handful of NDAs to thousands of commercial agreements.

## How we compared the tools

We used five criteria:

1. **Playbook control** – Can you build and maintain your own playbook, are pre-built playbooks available, and does the vendor charge to build it?
2. **Workspace** – Does review happen in Microsoft Word with tracked changes, in a web app, or both?
3. **Approval and audit trail** – Is there a defined sign-off step and a record of AI suggestions versus human decisions?
4. **Integration** – Does it work with your existing systems, or assume contracts move into a new platform such as a contract lifecycle management (CLM) system?
5. **Pricing transparency and buying process** – Is pricing published, and can you start self-serve or is a demo required?

The order below is not a quality score. It reflects how much verifiable public detail we could find against these criteria, and we have deliberately not placed ourselves first.

**Who it suits:** In-house legal teams that want a fast start with pre-built playbooks and the option to customise.

## Which option suits which team?

**Solo practitioners and small teams.** Published, self-serve pricing matters most. Gavel Exec stands out on transparency; Spellbook is an option if you want Word-native drafting and are comfortable with a quote.

**In-house teams handling high volumes.** LegalOn's pre-built playbooks and jurisdiction add-on, BlackBoiler's high-volume automation and Ivo's benchmarking are all worth a trial or demo. Ask each how reviewer decisions are logged.

**Enterprises that need governance across teams.** If the question is not only how to redline faster but how to prove who approved what, and how agreed terms reach finance and operations, a governed platform is the better fit. This is where STIV AI is designed to sit: playbook-based review with approval gates, audit trails and hand-offs to other division agents, on top of the systems you already run.

## How to choose: a short step list

1. **Write down your playbook status.** If it exists in a document, check how easily each tool ingests it; if not, decide whether you will build it or pay a vendor.
2. **Run the same three contracts through each shortlisted tool.** Compare redline quality and whether each change is explained.
3. **Test sign-off.** Ask who can approve a redline before it goes out and where that decision is recorded.
4. **Check integration.** Confirm whether contracts must move into a new system.
5. **Verify pricing on the vendor's own page**, including add-ons and minimum commitments.
