## What does an AI sales assistant approval workflow actually mean?

Many products say they keep a "human in the loop." That phrase covers very different setups. A workflow that holds up to scrutiny has four distinct stages.

### Draft, review, approve, send

1. **Draft.** The assistant writes the follow-up, using deal context such as recent emails, meeting notes, stage and next steps.
2. **Review.** A named person sees the draft, the reasoning behind it and the recipient before anything is sent.
3. **Approve or edit.** The reviewer approves, edits or rejects the draft. The decision is tied to their identity.
4. **Send and record.** The message goes out only after approval. The draft, the edits, the approver and a timestamp are all retained.

### Approval gates vs. autopilot modes

An **approval gate** is a hard stop: the system cannot carry out the action until someone signs off. An **autopilot mode** lets the AI act on its own, sometimes within limits such as sequence rules or send caps. Many tools offer both. The governance question is who can switch between them, and whether that switch is itself recorded.

### Why audit trails matter

- **Compliance:** regulated industries and cross-border sales often need proof of who said what to a customer, and when.
- **Brand risk:** one poorly judged automated message to a key account can undo months of relationship work.
- **Accountability:** when a deal goes wrong, leaders need to know whether the AI or a person made the call.

## How to set up an approval workflow with any tool

The principles are the same whichever vendor you choose.

1. **Classify actions by consequence.** Drafting and internal summaries may need no approval. Customer-facing emails, pricing statements and contract terms should.
2. **Name approvers and escalation paths.** Decide who approves by deal size, region or account tier, and who steps in when they are unavailable.
3. **Lock the default.** Make review-before-send the standard. Restrict and log any exceptions.
4. **Define stalled-deal rules.** Agree what counts as stalled, such as days without activity or overdue next steps, so flags lead to action rather than noise.
5. **Review the audit log regularly.** Look at edit rates, rejection reasons and response times. A high edit rate means the drafts need better context. A near-zero edit rate may mean reviewers are approving without reading.
6. **Extend deliberately.** Once sales approvals work, apply the same model to finance and legal actions instead of adding separate, inconsistent controls.

### Can AI flag stalled deals automatically?

Yes, many sales assistants can surface deals that show signs of stalling, such as long periods without activity or overdue next steps. Accuracy depends on the quality and completeness of your CRM and activity data. Agree internally on what counts as stalled so the flags are useful.

### What does human-in-the-loop mean for sales AI?

It means a person reviews and approves AI output before it takes effect. In a strong setup this is a hard approval gate, not an optional setting, and the approver, edits and timestamp are recorded.
