Agentforce's pipeline agent can run in a suggestive mode where the rep approves each proposed update, and HubSpot offers a "Review before sending" setting ([AutomationHero](https://automationhero.ai/blog/tips-using-sales-ai-assistant/)). ## Key takeaways

- **Approval is more than a send button.** Ask who can approve what, whether rules can vary by deal size or risk, and whether every draft, edit and approval is logged.
- **Salesforce and HubSpot document approval modes.** Agentforce offers suggestive and autonomous modes, and HubSpot offers "Review before sending".
- **Gong and Clari lean towards analysis and forecasting. Outreach and Salesloft lean towards sequencing.** Verify draft-and-approve behaviour directly with each vendor.
- **Keep humans on high-stakes sends.** Pricing, contract terms, high-value deals and first contact should stay approval-gated until accuracy is proven.
- **STIV AI suits enterprises** that want approval-gated agents across sales, finance, legal and operations without migrating data.

## What does "AI sales assistant with approval" actually mean?

Most roundups treat approval as a single checkbox. monday.com, for example, notes that most customer-facing communications can be configured to require human approval before sending. That is a useful baseline, but enterprise buyers need more precision.

### Suggestive vs autonomous modes

The cleanest example is Salesforce. According to [AutomationHero](https://automationhero.ai/blog/tips-using-sales-ai-assistant/), its pipeline agent runs in **suggestive mode**, where the rep reviews and approves each proposed update, or **autonomous mode**, where it writes changes directly. Both modes show the source of each change. Confirm the current naming in Salesforce's own documentation, as product terms change often.

### Approval per send vs approval rules

Per-send approval means a rep clicks "approve" on every message. Approval *rules* go further. They route a draft to a manager when it mentions a discount, to legal when it touches contract terms, or straight through when it is a routine meeting confirmation. Few public comparisons address this, yet it is what separates a pilot from a production deployment.

### Audit trails: who drafted, who edited, who approved

Apollo argues that governance belongs in the integration spec, not after it. As AI agents take autonomous actions such as sending emails, updating CRM fields and scheduling meetings, you need permissioning, audit trails and approval gates before going live ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/how-do-i-integrate-an-ai-sales-assistant-into-my-existing-outbound-workflow)). A proper audit trail answers three questions: what the AI drafted, what a human changed, and who signed off.

## How to judge an approval-first sales assistant

Use these seven criteria when shortlisting:

1. **Drafts follow-ups.** Does it write the email, or only recommend that you send one?
2. **Flags stalled deals.** Does it surface deals that have gone quiet or show risk signals?
3. **Requires approval before sending.** Can you enforce review, not just allow it?
4. **Approval rules.** Can approval vary by deal value, recipient, content or risk?
5. **Audit log.** Is every draft, edit and approval recorded and retrievable?
6. **Works with existing systems.** Does it connect to your current CRM and email, or require migration?
7. **Works across departments.** Can a sales draft hand off to legal or finance for approval inside the same workflow?

## Which sends should always need approval?

Even the best assistant will occasionally misjudge tone or facts. Crono advises keeping a human approval step on high-value and sensitive sends until tone and accuracy are proven ([Crono](https://www.crono.one/academy/ai-follow-up-emails/)). In practice, keep approval mandatory for:

- **High-value deals**, where one poorly worded message can cost a quarter's target.
- **Pricing, discounts and contract terms**, which carry commercial and legal exposure.
- **Regulated or sensitive data**, including anything subject to privacy obligations.
- **First contact with a new account**, where first impressions are hard to repair.
- **Escalations and complaints**, where empathy and accountability matter most.

Lower-risk messages such as meeting confirmations or resource links can graduate to lighter-touch review once your audit log shows consistent quality. That decision should be deliberate and recorded, not drift by default.
