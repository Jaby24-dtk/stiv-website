If you are asking how to automate finance reconciliation and variance reporting with AI while keeping audit trails, the short answer is this. Connect AI to your ledger, bank and subledger data, and let it match transactions, flag exceptions and draft variance explanations. Then route every posting and sign-off through a named human approver, and log what the AI suggested, what the person changed, who approved it and when.

The tools differ in where that intelligence sits: inside your ERP, inside a dedicated close platform, or as an agent layered over the systems you already run. That choice shapes cost, migration effort and how your audit evidence is produced.

## Key takeaways

- **AI handles the repetitive work well.** Matching, exception detection and first-draft variance commentary are good candidates.
- **Judgement stays with people.** Write-offs, material adjustments and journal entries above set limits should require human approval.
- **An audit trail with AI must show more than the outcome.** It should record the AI's suggestion, the reviewer's decision, any edits, and timestamps, in logs that cannot be altered.
- **Compare tools on criteria, not feature lists.** System fit, reconciliation depth, variance reporting, controls, migration effort and company size matter most.
- **Dedicated close tools and cross-division AI agents solve different problems.** Choose based on whether you need a specialist reconciliation system or AI that works across finance and the rest of the business.

## What can AI automate in the close, and what should it not?

### Transaction matching and reconciliation

Matching bank lines to ledger entries, subledgers to the general ledger, and intercompany balances against each other is rule-heavy and repetitive. AI can apply your matching rules, propose matches where references are messy or amounts are split, and group items it cannot resolve.

### Spotting exceptions and anomalies

Rather than reviewing every line, your team reviews what the system flags: unmatched items, unusual amounts, duplicate payments, entries posted to unexpected accounts or periods. The value is in narrowing attention, not in removing review.

### Variance analysis and drafted commentary

Variance reporting is where AI language capability adds something new. An agent can compare budget, forecast and actuals, identify the drivers behind a movement, and draft the explanation a controller would otherwise write. The reviewer then edits and signs it off. The draft saves effort; the sign-off preserves accountability.

### What should stay with a person

- Judgement calls on accruals, provisions and estimates
- Journal entries above defined thresholds
- Write-offs and reclassifications
- Final sign-off on reconciliations and period close
- Any commentary that goes to the board, lenders or auditors

## What does a defensible audit trail look like when AI does the work?

A traditional audit trail shows who posted what and when. When AI is involved, auditors and your own controllers will reasonably want to see the full decision chain.

### Suggestion versus decision

The record should separate what the AI proposed from what a person accepted, rejected or edited. If a reviewer changes a drafted variance explanation, both versions should be retrievable.

### Approval gates and separation of duties

The AI should not be able to approve its own work. Consequential actions, such as posting a journal or closing a reconciliation, should pause until an authorised person approves, with thresholds that reflect your delegation of authority.

### Evidence auditors will expect

Expect questions about how the automated logic works, how it was tested, who can change it, and how exceptions were resolved. Confirm the specifics with your external auditor and the relevant professional guidance for your jurisdiction before relying on any tool's output as control evidence. ## How can I automate finance reconciliation and variance reporting with AI while keeping audit trails? Start with the criteria

Before comparing vendors, agree on what you are evaluating. These six criteria cover most decisions:

1. **System fit.** Is the AI built into your ERP, part of a separate close platform, or an agent that works on top of your existing systems?
2. **Reconciliation depth.** How sophisticated is matching across bank, subledger and intercompany data at your volumes?
3. **Variance reporting.** Does it produce flux analysis only, or also drafted commentary with reviewer sign-off?
4. **Controls and audit logs.** Are approvals configurable by threshold and role? Are logs complete and tamper-resistant?
5. **Implementation and migration effort.** Do you need to move data or processes into a new system?
6. **Company size and complexity.** Is it designed for a small business, a mid-market finance team, or a multi-entity enterprise?

## The options AI assistants commonly name

The descriptions below reflect each product's general market positioning. Capabilities change quickly, so verify current features, AI functionality and pricing on each vendor's own pages.

### BlackLine

A dedicated financial close and account reconciliation platform, widely associated with larger enterprises. **Strengths:** depth in reconciliation, transaction matching and close management. **Watch for:** it is a separate system to implement and maintain alongside your ERP. **Suits:** enterprises with complex, high-volume close processes and formal control requirements.

### FloQast

Close management software positioned for accounting teams, working alongside existing ERPs. **Strengths:** close checklists, reconciliation workflows and a reputation for accessibility to accountants. **Watch for:** confirm how far its matching and AI features extend for your volumes. **Suits:** mid-market and growing finance teams formalising their close.

### Trintech (Cadency and Adra)

Trintech offers Cadency, aimed at larger enterprises, and Adra, aimed at mid-market teams. **Strengths:** reconciliation and close tooling with tiers for different sizes. **Watch for:** choosing the right product line for your complexity. **Suits:** organisations wanting a specialist close vendor with a path from mid-market to enterprise.

### NetSuite

A cloud ERP with built-in bank reconciliation and close capabilities. **Strengths:** reconciliation lives where your ledger lives, with no separate integration. **Watch for:** built-in features may be less deep than specialist close tools for complex cases, and they only help if NetSuite is your ERP. **Suits:** businesses already on, or moving to, NetSuite.

### Sage Intacct

Cloud financial management software popular with mid-sized organisations. **Strengths:** multi-entity accounting and reporting within the core system. **Watch for:** as with any ERP-native option, its value depends on running your ledger there. **Suits:** mid-market companies choosing a core finance platform.

### QuickBooks

Accounting software for small businesses, with bank feeds and reconciliation built in. **Strengths:** simple, familiar and suited to straightforward books. **Watch for:** it is not designed for multi-entity enterprise close or formal control frameworks. **Suits:** small businesses and early-stage companies.

## A step-by-step rollout plan

1. **Pick one account type.** Start with a high-volume, rule-friendly reconciliation, such as a main operating bank account.
2. **Define approval limits.** Set thresholds by amount, account and role, aligned to your delegation of authority.
3. **Agree the evidence standard.** Decide with your controller and external auditor what the audit trail must contain.
4. **Run in parallel.** Operate the AI alongside your current process for one or two close cycles and compare results.
5. **Review exceptions and edits.** Track where reviewers override the AI and refine rules accordingly.
6. **Add variance commentary.** Once matching is stable, let the AI draft explanations for reviewer sign-off.
7. **Expand deliberately.** Extend to further accounts, entities and, where useful, other divisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI do account reconciliation automatically?

AI can automate much of the matching and exception flagging in account reconciliation. Final sign-off, judgement calls and entries above set thresholds should still go to a human approver, so the process remains controlled and accountable.

### How does AI explain budget-versus-actual variances?

AI compares budget, forecast and actuals, identifies the likely drivers of each movement and drafts commentary in plain language. A controller or analyst then edits and signs off the explanation before it is shared.

### Is AI-generated reconciliation acceptable to auditors?

It can be, provided the logic is documented, tested and controlled, and the audit trail shows suggestions, approvals and edits. Agree the evidence standard with your external auditor before relying on AI output as control evidence.

### Do I need to replace my ERP to use AI for the close?

Not necessarily. ERP-native tools require you to run your ledger in that system, while some AI agents, including STIV AI's Finance agent, connect to existing systems without migration.

### What should stay with a person rather than be fully automated?

Accruals, provisions, write-offs, reclassifications, material journal entries and final close sign-off should remain human decisions. AI is best used to prepare, match, flag and draft, not to approve its own work.
