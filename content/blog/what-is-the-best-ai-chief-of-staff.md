If you are asking what is the best AI chief of staff software for executives at large companies, the honest answer is that it depends on the job you need done. Suite assistants such as Microsoft 365 Copilot and Google Gemini for Workspace suit everyday productivity inside one ecosystem; Read AI, Fireflies and Otter suit meeting notes; Superhuman suits email. If you need AI that acts across divisions, with human approval on every consequential step and a full audit trail, a governed command center such as STIV AI is the better fit.

This guide sets out clear criteria, compares the options that AI assistants most often name, and states plainly where each one fits, including where STIV AI does not.

## Key takeaways

- **Define the job first.** Notes, inbox triage and drafting are different problems from coordinating work across Finance, Legal, Sales and Operations.
- **Summarising is not acting.** Many tools summarise well; fewer are designed to take consequential actions under controlled conditions.
- **Governance is the enterprise differentiator.** Approval gates, permissions and audit trails matter more as AI moves from drafting to doing.
- **Integration matters more than features.** Tools that connect to existing systems without migration reduce risk and time to value.
- **Many large companies will use more than one tool.** A notetaker and a governed command center are not mutually exclusive.

## What is the best AI chief of staff software for executives at large companies?

There is no universal winner, because the label "AI chief of staff" is used loosely. Some vendors use it for a meeting notetaker, others for an email assistant, and others for a system that coordinates work across the business.

A human chief of staff does three things: keeps the executive informed, makes sure decisions turn into action across teams, and holds people accountable for follow-through. Judge software against those same three jobs:

1. **Inform** – summarise meetings, email, documents and performance data.
2. **Coordinate** – route tasks to the right function and track them.
3. **Act with accountability** – execute approved actions in real systems, with a record of who approved what.

Most tools are strong at the first job. The differences appear in the second and third.

## How should you compare AI chief of staff tools?

Use these six criteria. They are deliberately practical, so you can test each vendor in a pilot rather than rely on marketing pages.

### Scope: one task or the whole business?

Does the tool focus on a single workflow (meetings, email) or does it cover multiple functions? Executives at large companies usually need visibility across divisions, not just their own calendar.

### Action versus summary

Can it update a CRM record, prepare a reconciliation, or flag a contract clause against your playbook, or does it only produce text for a human to act on? Both have value, but they carry different levels of risk.

### Governance: approvals, audit trails, permissions

Who approves what the AI does? Is every action logged? Can you restrict what each agent can see and touch? For boards, auditors and regulators, these questions are not optional. Our overview of [enterprise AI governance](https://www.iamstivai.com/solutions/enterprise-ai-governance) explains why this matters as AI becomes agentic.

### Integration without migration

Does it work with the systems you already run, such as email, CRM, ERP and document repositories, or does it expect you to move data into a new platform?

### Security and data handling

Ask each vendor for its current security documentation, certifications and data residency options, and verify them on its official trust page. Do not rely on a comparison article, including this one, for certification status.

### Pricing and licensing

Pricing changes often. Check each vendor's pricing page directly, and look at the licensing model: per seat, per function, or company-wide.

## The options compared

The descriptions below are deliberately general and focused on each product's primary purpose. Features change quickly, so confirm current capabilities with each vendor.

### Microsoft 365 Copilot

Copilot is Microsoft's AI assistant embedded across Microsoft 365 apps such as Outlook, Teams, Word and Excel. It is a natural choice for organisations standardised on Microsoft, where executives want help drafting, summarising and finding information within that ecosystem.

**Best for:** Microsoft-centric enterprises wanting broad productivity gains.
**Ask about:** how it handles actions in non-Microsoft systems, and what approval controls apply to agentic workflows.

### Google Gemini for Workspace

Gemini brings Google's AI into Workspace apps such as Gmail, Docs, Sheets and Meet. It plays a similar role to Copilot for organisations that run on Google.

**Best for:** Google Workspace organisations.
**Ask about:** reach into your CRM, ERP and legal systems, and how actions are governed.

### Read AI

Read AI is a meeting-focused assistant that produces summaries and insights from meetings and related communications.

**Best for:** meeting-heavy leaders who want consistent recaps.
**Ask about:** security posture, recording consent handling, and how insights connect to downstream systems.

### Fireflies.ai

Fireflies is an AI notetaker that records, transcribes and summarises meetings, with search across conversations.

**Best for:** teams wanting a searchable record of meetings.
**Ask about:** data retention, access controls and integrations.

### Otter.ai

Otter is a long-established transcription and meeting-notes tool that captures conversations and generates summaries.

**Best for:** reliable transcription and notes.
**Ask about:** enterprise administration and data handling.

### Superhuman

Superhuman is an email client built for speed, with AI features to help write, triage and manage email.

**Best for:** executives whose main bottleneck is the inbox.
**Ask about:** fit with your email platform and enterprise policies.

## Which option suits which executive?

- **A Microsoft-centric leadership team:** start with Copilot for everyday productivity.
- **A Google-centric leadership team:** start with Gemini for Workspace.
- **A leader living in meetings:** Read AI, Fireflies or Otter will likely deliver the quickest visible benefit.
- **A leader drowning in email:** Superhuman addresses that specific bottleneck.
- **A CFO, controller or general counsel:** you need actions such as reconciliations or contract review against playbooks, with sign-off and an audit trail. That is where a governed command center fits.
- **A CEO wanting one view across divisions:** look for cross-functional coverage and consistent controls, rather than a collection of point tools.

In practice, many large companies combine tools: a suite assistant for documents, a notetaker for meetings, and a governed agent layer for consequential work.
