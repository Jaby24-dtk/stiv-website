## What does "reviewing against your playbook" actually mean?

GC AI offers a useful plain definition: AI redlining is [a first-pass review against your instructions or contract playbook, with proposed edits for a lawyer to assess](https://gc.ai/blog/contract-redlining-software). Two parts of that sentence matter: *your* playbook, and a *lawyer* assessing the output.

### Playbook rules, fallback positions and approved wording

A usable playbook typically contains:

- **Standard positions** for each clause type (liability caps, indemnities, governing law, payment terms).
- **Fallback positions** you will accept if the counterparty pushes back.
- **Approved wording** the AI can insert rather than inventing language.
- **Escalation thresholds** that define when a deviation needs a senior lawyer or a commercial owner.

### Flagging clauses versus drafting tracked-change redlines

Some tools only flag risky or non-standard clauses. Others draft tracked-change redlines with suggested replacement text. Flagging is a lighter lift, while redlining saves more time but raises the bar for accuracy and explainability. Confirm which one you are buying.

## How should you evaluate the options?

Use the same criteria for every vendor so the comparison is fair.

1. **Where it works.** Is it a Word add-in, a web upload tool, or part of a contract lifecycle management (CLM) system?
2. **How your playbook is loaded.** Can you encode your own rules, or do you start from vendor templates? Can it learn positions from past executed contracts?
3. **Explainability.** Can a reviewer see which playbook rule triggered each redline? LegalSifter puts this well: its ReviewPro product [produces an auditable redline tied to a documented playbook rule](https://www.legalsifter.com/products/ai-contract-review/).
4. **Human approval and audit trail.** Who signs off on each change, and is that decision recorded?
5. **Security and data handling.** Which certifications does the vendor hold, and is your data used for model training? Get answers in writing.
6. **Pricing transparency.** Is pricing published, or quote-only?
7. **Fit with other systems and teams.** Do agreed terms reach the people who act on them, such as sales, finance and operations?

## How do you get your playbook ready for AI review?

Whichever tool you choose, output quality depends on the playbook. Before a pilot:

1. **List your clause types** and write one standard position for each.
2. **Add fallback positions** in order of preference.
3. **Store approved wording** so the AI inserts language you have already vetted.
4. **Set escalation thresholds**, such as liability above a certain cap or non-standard governing law.
5. **Assign approvers** by clause type and deal value.
6. **Decide where the record lives**, so every accepted or rejected redline can be traced to a rule and a person.
7. **Test on past contracts** where you already know the right answer, and compare the AI's redlines to your lawyers' choices.
